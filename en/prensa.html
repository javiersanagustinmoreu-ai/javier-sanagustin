Short biography

Javier Sanagustín Moreu (Huesca, 1995) is a painter and director of the Escuela de Dibujo y Pintura Sanagustín. His training lies outside the academy: he trained in the classroom, teaching and watching his students work. His work evolves from realism toward a gestural language, where stain and water carry the emotion of each painting.

Q&A

Translated from the Spanish. Translation pending the artist’s review.

Don’t you look for a single style?
I don’t pursue a single style that identifies me. Each work asks for its own form, and that variety is my way of looking for emotion, not of fitting myself into a style. It is a decision, not a doubt.

What did you learn from the great masters?
I didn’t go to learn from them, in the sense of studying their technique. I went to recognise something that was already in me. Those paintings were a trigger, something that activated what I already carried inside. And the result has been different in each work: some came out very close to the original, almost faithful, and those are the reflections. Others came out transformed, harder to trace back to the source, and those are the shadows.

Where does Reflejos y sombras come from?
It comes from a visit to the Prado. I heard a boy say to his father, let’s go and see Goya. He said it like someone going to see somebody close, somebody who is still there.
